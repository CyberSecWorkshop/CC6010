01 · BEFORE YOU BEGIN — METHOD AND SAFETY

The methodology follows the sequence used in digital open-source investigations: define the question, identify sources, collect records, preserve what was observed, verify with independent material, analyse and report. Public availability does not remove requirements for lawful collection or appropriate handling.

Define the investigation question.

Record what you need to establish before opening a search tool.

Capture source provenance.

For every key result, save the URL, title, retrieval time in UTC, observed data and evidence identifier.

Verify independently.

Cross-check records using another source or another type of evidence; avoid counting copies of the same original as independent confirmation.

Explain limitations.

Dynamic registration and certificate records can change. Archival gaps, map alignment and approximate coordinates create uncertainty.

Authorisation boundary
Passive public-source research only. Do not scan or probe websites, attempt logins, access restricted records, contact people, or download sensitive material. bellingcat.com is used as a legitimate public website for technical research; no wrongdoing is alleged.
Read this before using the HTML
Your typed answers and evidence rows are stored in this browser only when local storage is available. They are not sent to a server. Use Export findings at the end before switching computers or clearing browser data. External websites and challenge images require an internet connection.
02 · EVIDENCE RECORD — SET IT UP FIRST

Assign an evidence identifier to each record. A browser screenshot without a source URL and access time is difficult to interpret or repeat. You can enter at least four records below and export them with your final report.

MINIMUM EVIDENCE LOG FIELDS
  • Source: original URL or persistent record identifier.
  • Observation: the specific information actually returned or shown.
  • Time: when you viewed it, recorded in UTC. Distinguish the retrieval time from a webpage or certificate's own event date.
  • Confidence: confidence in the interpretation, supported by a reason.

STAGE A · PUBLIC WEBSITE TECHNICAL FOOTPRINT · 20 MIN
// Case A · Legitimate public website · 8 marks

Subject: the public-facing domain bellingcat.com, belonging to Bellingcat, an open-source investigation organisation. Your task is to collect publicly observable technical and historical records from third-party services.

Question: What information can be established about the domain's public registration, certificate history and website archive, and how are these observations connected?

A1 · RDAPICANN Lookup — registration data

RDAP stands for Registration Data Access Protocol. It returns structured domain registration information from participating registration data services. It may include registrar information, dates and domain status codes; personal contact information may be redacted or unavailable.

Open ICANN LookupWhat RDAP fields mean
SEARCH THIS DOMAINbellingcat.com
Search for the domain.

Enter the exact domain name in ICANN Lookup. Note whether a usable record is returned.

Record two actual fields.

For example: registrar, one domain status code, or a reported registration/update date. Record field labels exactly as displayed.

Identify one visibility limit.

Which data is redacted or absent? Record access date in UTC and explain why absence of a public field is inconclusive.

QUALITY CHECK
  • Do not invent the registrar or a registration date from memory. Record what the live service returns.
  • A domain registration entry does not identify who controls all services hosted behind the domain.
A2 · CTcrt.sh — public certificate-transparency records

Certificate transparency is a system of public logs recording certificates submitted to participating logs. A certificate entry can supply a date, issuer and DNS names covered, but cannot alone prove that a service is currently deployed or operated by a specific person.

Search crt.sh for domain
QUERY PATTERN%.bellingcat.com
Inspect one actual result.

Note a record identifier, date, issuer, common name or subject alternative name; available fields vary.

Explain its evidential meaning.

State what the certificate observation establishes and one conclusion it cannot support.

Live record caveat
If crt.sh is unreachable or does not return a useful result, log the URL, query, time and outcome. An accurately documented unavailable result is assessable; do not manufacture a certificate.
A3 · ARCHIVEInternet Archive — Wayback Machine

The Wayback Machine provides saved captures of selected webpages. A capture reflects what the archiving system recorded at that time, subject to incomplete collection, blocked resources and rendering differences.

Open Bellingcat archive calendar
Pick an archived capture.

Record the exact archived URL and its capture timestamp; do not confuse that date with today's retrieval date.

Describe what is visible.

Choose a checkable feature such as a visible heading, navigation item or story title.

Explain uncertainty.

What might be missing in an archived capture? Does a missing capture establish that a page did not exist?

A4 · GRAPHMaltego-style relationship analysis — document each edge

Maltego uses entities and transforms to discover and visualise relationships. An entity represents an observable item, such as a domain, certificate or webpage. A transform retrieves or derives related entities from a data source. A relationship in a graph must still be checked against its original supporting record.

This browser exercise gives you a small evidence graph without requiring Maltego installation. Enter the certificate record and archived capture you actually observed. You may reproduce the graph in Maltego if you have access to it.

Maltego transforms reference
WHY GRAPH EDGES NEED SOURCES
  • In Maltego, a transform output is an investigative lead until the underlying record is examined.
  • Different domains can share infrastructure; an edge does not automatically establish ownership, intent or compromise.
Lecturer guidance / self-check for Stage A — open only after attempting

A1: accept any two real fields from the current ICANN lookup, with source and access time. Fields can change or be redacted. Do not require a fixed registrar or timestamp.

A2: accept a record identifier, covered DNS name or issuer supported by the actual crt.sh result; a certificate entry is evidence of a logged certificate, not current ownership or malicious use. A documented unavailable result can be accepted.

A3: accept a valid archived capture with date and one directly visible feature. Missing captures or scripts cannot establish non-existence of a webpage.

A4: a graph must link Domain → Current website, Domain → CT observation, and Domain/website → archived capture, with each edge linked to a checkable record. Maltego is optional. Total for Stage A: 8 marks.

Stage A complete?
Ensure the four observations have source URLs and times before proceeding.
STAGE B · GEOLOCATE AN AUTHENTIC PHOTO · 20 MIN
// Case B · Gralhix OSINT Exercise 004 · 8 marks

Inspect a genuine aerial photograph of an island resort. Establish the resort's name, approximate coordinates and the direction the camera was facing. Use reverse-image search to generate leads, then independently match the island's outline and neighbouring islands using satellite mapping.

Original challenge photograph

Original OSINT Exercise 004 image via Sofia Santos (Gralhix). The author notes she does not own the photograph; it is referenced here for educational investigation. If the image does not load, open the original exercise link below.

Original Exercise 004Google LensGoogle Earth

Investigation roadmap

1. Describe the visual clues before searching.

Record island outline, the reef and lagoon colours, visible resort structures, and the arrangement of distant islands. These provide landmarks independent of image-search text.

2. Search by image.

Open the original photograph in a separate tab. Use Google Lens or a reverse-image service. Treat a matching label as a hypothesis until cross-checked.

3. Verify the geographic match.

Locate the candidate resort in Google Maps or Earth. Compare shoreline, adjacent islets and visible infrastructure; record approximate coordinates from the map.

4. Reconstruct camera orientation.

Rotate north-up satellite imagery and compare the positions of the distant islands relative to the foreground island. Determine the approximate cardinal direction.

Instructor marking distinction
An approximate coordinate near the island centre or the resort is acceptable if the location and method are justified. A precise-looking coordinate copied without a source is weaker than a properly documented approximate location.
Reveal published Exercise 004 solution and method (after attempting)

Location: Oan Resort, Oan Island, Wonip, Chuuk State, Federated States of Micronesia. Approximate coordinates: 7.363080° N, 151.755930° E (published walkthrough; a second walkthrough uses 7.3625207, 151.7561042 for a nearby map pin). Camera facing: roughly north-west.

Evidence: reverse-image search identifies the named resort; matching island shape and distant islets in a satellite map corroborate the place; compass orientation supports north-west.

Independent published methods: Synoslabs walkthrough and David Varghese walkthrough. The original photograph remains on Gralhix.

Evidential limitation: map-marker and island-centre coordinates differ slightly, and the view direction is approximate.

Stage B complete?
Include the resort identification and at least one independent geographic cross-check.
STAGE C · VERIFY HISTORICAL SATELLITE IMAGERY · 20 MIN
// Case C · Gralhix OSINT Exercise 012 · 9 marks

An authentic image shows a coastal region with a thermal-anomaly layer. Establish which data platform produced the view, which country is shown and the date represented. The image depicts a historical event; you are not assessing a current fire.

Original thermal-anomaly image

Original image and task by Sofia Santos (Gralhix), OSINT Exercise 012. A red mark is a reported remotely sensed thermal anomaly, not independently a finding about cause or exact damage extent.

Original Exercise 012NASA FIRMS Fire MapOpenStreetMap

Investigation roadmap

1. Analyse the screenshot interface.

Identify the base map style, red anomaly overlay, visible scale and interface controls. Compare with public satellite fire-monitoring tools.

2. Identify the coastline.

Use the position of the ocean, coast outline and inland features to compare with a map. A country guess based on vegetation alone is insufficient.

3. Reconstruct the historical date.

Search documented January fire events for the candidate region. Use the original platform's historical controls and credible contemporaneous reporting where possible.

4. Record what the layer can establish.

Explain the difference between a detected heat signal and a verified perimeter, cause, total damage or person responsible.

INTERPRETING REMOTELY SENSED OBSERVATIONS
  • A thermal anomaly is a detection under the sensor's observation and processing conditions; it is not a precise delineation of all burnt land.
  • Source date, satellite overpass time, map selection and spatial resolution can affect interpretation.
Reveal published Exercise 012 solution and method (after attempting)

Platform: NASA FIRMS, Fire Information for Resource Management System. Country: Chile. Date: 26 January 2017, according to independent published walkthroughs.

Method: the screenshot's presentation is matched to NASA FIRMS; coastal geography is compared with Chilean map data; historical fire reporting and archived FIRMS controls support the January 2017 date.

Independent walkthroughs: Legion Soul and Donovan Engelmann. They are independent write-ups of the same original challenge, not substitute primary satellite records.

Limitation: the image alone does not establish ignition cause, casualties, total burnt area or an exact recording time without checking source settings and historical data.

Stage C complete?
Include the platform, country, date and a clear description of how each was checked.
STAGE D · WRITE THE FORENSIC FINDING · 10 MIN

Choose either Stage B or Stage C. Write one professional finding of 150–220 words. The objective is to make a traceable, qualified conclusion with appropriate scope.

STRUCTUREQuestion → observation → reasoning

State the question, what the original source shows and why the corroborating material supports your interpretation.

EVIDENCE QUALITYSource → confidence → limitation

Include at least two source links, a confidence assessment and the most important remaining uncertainty.

0 words · target 150–220
Marking standard
2 marks: a correctly qualified conclusion; 1 mark: two traceable citations; 1 mark: calibrated confidence; 1 mark: an explicit evidence limitation. Do not claim identity, attribution or cause without supporting data.
Stage D complete?
Make sure your text contains the sources and their role in supporting the conclusion.
